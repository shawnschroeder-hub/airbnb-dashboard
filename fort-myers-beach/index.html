The bottom line

A great beach house. A tough investment at $675K.

Paul — the house itself checks out: solid 1962 concrete block that made it through Ian, updated interior, sold furnished, no HOA, and a real walk-to-beach location in the weekly-rental part of the island. But run as an Airbnb, a realistic year is about $50,900 of rental revenue, and after taxes, insurance, utilities and upkeep the property nets about $24,300 a year before any mortgage. Finance it with 20% down at today's ~7.5% investment rates and it loses about $1,750 every month. Pay all cash and it earns about a 3.5% return — it pays its own bills and throws off some income, but the return is modest for $700K+ of cash. The numbers only turn positive with financing somewhere near $400–425K, which is far below asking. If you want it as a family beach house that mostly pays for itself when you're not there, it works. As a pure money-maker, it doesn't — not at this price.

The make-or-break question

Confirm the weekly-rental status in writing — before anything else

Paul, you called this one right: on Fort Myers Beach it's all about the weekly zone. The town's default rule for residential areas is one rental per month (minimum one-week stay). Weekly rentals are only allowed for properties between Estero Blvd and the Gulf, bay-side properties adjacent to Estero Blvd, and homes with grandfathered weekly registrations from before Jan 1, 2003. The listing markets this house as a "weekly rental property," and other Fairweather Ln listings say the same — but this analysis assumes weekly status, and every revenue number on this page depends on it.

  • If weekly: the $50.9K/yr revenue estimate stands (≈27 weekly stays).
  • If monthly-only: you're capped at 12 stays a year — revenue roughly halves, and the deal is dead as a rental.
  • Verify: Town of Fort Myers Beach Community Development — str@fmbgov.com · (239) 765-0202. Ask specifically: does 216 Fairweather Ln hold a weekly rental registration, and does it transfer to a new owner? Get it in writing. All rentals must also register with the town (Rentalscape portal) and follow the STR Code of Conduct.

The pool argument is real (it's the difference between the two comp classes), but it strengthens a well-bought house — it can't rescue a full-price one. Costs & risks unique to this one

Eyes-open items

  • Flood zone AE (high-risk). Flood insurance is required by any lender. Estimated $1,200–5,000/yr depending on elevation — this analysis carries $2,500. Ask the seller for the elevation certificate and whether their flood policy is assumable (older policies can carry cheaper locked-in rates).
  • Wind insurance. Barrier-island wind coverage on a 1962 block home is estimated at ~$4,500/yr here; quotes can vary widely. Total insurance modeled: $7,000/yr ($5K–12K range). Get real quotes before offering.
  • Taxes reset. The listing's $7,059 becomes ≈$8,800 for a new buyer when Lee County reassesses at the sale price.
  • It survived Ian — that's genuinely worth something. Concrete block, still standing after the worst surge event in island history, in a town where 31% of rental supply is still gone. Scarcity is part of why winter rates are strong.
  • Guest quarters upside. The detached living space adds sleeping capacity (helps rate), but on FMB it almost certainly cannot be rented separately — rentals are limited to a single family per stay.
  • September–October is real hurricane season. The off-season comps show it: even pool homes drop under $2K/week, and some weeks simply don't book.
If you pursue it

Due-diligence checklist

  1. Written weekly-rental confirmation from the town (registration + transferability). Deal-breaker if it fails.
  2. Insurance quotes in hand before offering: wind + flood, with the elevation certificate; ask about assuming the seller's flood policy.
  3. Rental history: if it's operated as a rental, get 24 months of statements and the forward booking ledger.
  4. AirDNA Rentalizer report for this exact address (Shawn can run it) — a second opinion on the $50.9K estimate.
  5. Inspection focus: roof age (shingle), any Ian water intrusion history, and the guest quarters' permit status.
  6. Negotiate from the math: 84 days on market, one cut already, seller repricing at their own 2022 peak. The financed math says this house is worth fighting for in the low-to-mid $400Ks as an investment — anything above that is paying for the lifestyle, which is a fair choice if that's the goal.
